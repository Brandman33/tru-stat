Crime and immigration

What isn’t published, and why it’s hard to find

  • Police recorded crime figures don’t include the nationality or immigration status of suspects. A Home Office minister confirmed this in a written answer on 28 April 2026.
  • The Ministry of Justice publishes how many foreign nationals are in prison every quarter, but not convictions by nationality. It says prisoners’ immigration status is held by the Home Office, not by the Ministry.
  • On 22 June 2026 the Information Commissioner ordered the Ministry of Justice to release convictions by nationality for 2018 to 2024, after a Freedom of Information request. The Ministry appealed to the First-tier Tribunal on 17 July 2026. No ruling had been reported by mid-November.
  • In April 2025 the Home Office said it intends to publish more statistics on foreign national offenders once its data systems are upgraded.

How to check a claim

Same measure?

“Inflation” can mean CPI, CPIH or RPI. “Debt” can include or exclude the Bank of England. A claim is only comparable if it uses the same measure.

Same period?

A month, a quarter, a tax year (April to March) and a calendar year can all give different answers. Check the “Covers” line on each card.

Latest figure?

Official figures get revised. Check the “Latest data as of” date: an older number may have been updated since.

Who published it?

Every figure here comes from an official statistics body or government department, and links to the original. Party press releases, think tanks and campaigns are not used as sources.

The small print
  • DashUK shows official figures only, without commentary. It doesn’t take a side on any policy or party.
  • Every figure comes from an official source, linked under “What is this?” on its card.
  • A green dot on a date means the figure was fetched live from the ONS or the Bank of England when you opened the page. Everything else is the latest saved figure, refreshed whenever the site is updated.
  • Two figures are official Home Office numbers as reported by the PA news agency, because the official page couldn’t be read directly: small boats so far in 2026, and asylum seekers in hotels.
  • Many figures are first estimates and get revised, especially borrowing, debt, GDP and migration.
  • HMRC counts tax when the cash arrives; the ONS counts it when it’s owed. Their totals can differ.
  • Prison figures cover England and Wales. Health figures are for England.
  • Today’s talking points are short summaries written from Met Office and UKHSA releases.
  • Rings show a real share of a whole, or progress towards a stated target, never an arbitrary scale.
